Now that we better understand the nature&nbsp;
of sedimentary rocks, we are ready to&nbsp;&nbsp;
start classifying them. Sedimentary rocks&nbsp;
are grouped into three primary categories:&nbsp;&nbsp;
Terrigenous or siliciclastic, biogenic, and&nbsp;
chemogenic. Terrigenous, which literally&nbsp;&nbsp;
means “originating from Earth” from the Latin&nbsp;
terra, refers to materials derived from the&nbsp;&nbsp;
erosion of preexisting rocks. Siliciclastic&nbsp;
refers to materials composed of clasts,&nbsp;&nbsp;
or fragments of silicate minerals. Terrigenous&nbsp;
and siliciclastic are used synonymously since most&nbsp;&nbsp;
terrigenous sediments are derived from the erosion&nbsp;
of silicate rocks. Biogenic, or biochemical rocks,&nbsp;&nbsp;
are derived from living organisms, both&nbsp;
directly and indirectly. Chemogenic,&nbsp;&nbsp;
or chemical sedimentary rocks are derived from&nbsp;
the precipitation of minerals from natural waters,&nbsp;&nbsp;
usually involving evaporation in arid climates.
Let’s start with the siliciclastic sedimentary&nbsp;&nbsp;
rocks, since they are the most abundant,&nbsp;
accounting for roughly three-fourths of&nbsp;&nbsp;
all sedimentary rocks. In our study of igneous&nbsp;
rocks, we learned that one of the first steps in&nbsp;&nbsp;
analyzing a hand sample is to determine the grain&nbsp;
size, and the same holds true for sedimentary&nbsp;&nbsp;
rocks. In fact, the main way that geologists&nbsp;
classify siliciclastic rocks is based on grain&nbsp;&nbsp;
size. Recall from earlier that the grain size of&nbsp;
sediments is broken down into gravel, sand, silt,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and clay. So, a sandstone is a stone composed of&nbsp;
mainly sand-sized particles, while a claystone is&nbsp;&nbsp;
a stone composed mainly of clay-sized particles.&nbsp;
What about a rock that consists of a mix of sand,&nbsp;&nbsp;
silt, and clay? Geologists often use&nbsp;
ternary diagrams to systematically classify&nbsp;&nbsp;
rocks. Ternary diagrams represent the relative&nbsp;
proportions of three components, in this case,&nbsp;&nbsp;
different grain sizes. For example, this&nbsp;
ternary diagram has the components sand,&nbsp;&nbsp;
silt, and clay, with each corner of the triangle&nbsp;
representing 100% of one component. For example,&nbsp;&nbsp;
if you were to determine that a sedimentary&nbsp;
rock contains 60% sand, 20% silt, and 20% clay,&nbsp;&nbsp;
it would be classified as a muddy sandstone. It&nbsp;
is important to choose the appropriate ternary&nbsp;&nbsp;
diagram when classifying a rock. For example, you&nbsp;
would not use a ternary diagram with gravel as&nbsp;&nbsp;
a component for the aforementioned rock, since&nbsp;
it does not contain gravel. Furthermore, it is&nbsp;&nbsp;
important to recognize that the classification of&nbsp;
a rock as a sandstone, siltstone, or otherwise,&nbsp;&nbsp;
says nothing about its mineralogy, though&nbsp;
it is implied to be siliciclastic, mainly&nbsp;&nbsp;
because the biochemical and chemical rocks&nbsp;
follow a different classification scheme.&nbsp;
Before we explore the main types of siliciclastic&nbsp;
rocks, we must cover some terminology so we&nbsp;&nbsp;
can better describe them. Let’s use a typical&nbsp;
sandstone as an example. Sandstones consist mainly&nbsp;&nbsp;
of sand-sized grains of silicate minerals, often&nbsp;
quartz, which make up the framework portion of the&nbsp;&nbsp;
rock. Imagine you are building a stone wall, using&nbsp;
only the randomly shaped rocks found in a stream.&nbsp;&nbsp;
How would they fit together? Obviously, there will&nbsp;
be a good amount of empty space between the rocks,&nbsp;&nbsp;
owing to the fact that random shapes do not&nbsp;
perfectly fit together. The same applies to&nbsp;&nbsp;
the sand in a sandstone, there is an amount of&nbsp;
empty space between the sand grains due to the&nbsp;&nbsp;
way they fit together. This is known as a rock’s&nbsp;
pore space, and it is often filled with materials&nbsp;&nbsp;
to an extent. In some cases, it’s filled with&nbsp;
fine-grained muds, which is referred to as the&nbsp;&nbsp;
matrix portion of a rock. The matrix is commonly&nbsp;
composed of clay minerals, such as illite and&nbsp;&nbsp;
kaolinite. Other times, pores get filled in with&nbsp;
minerals that precipitate out of groundwater,&nbsp;&nbsp;
called cement. The cement helps glue the rock&nbsp;
together, making it harder and more cohesive.&nbsp;&nbsp;
The most common cement-forming minerals are&nbsp;
quartz and calcite, with lesser occurrences of&nbsp;&nbsp;
the iron oxide minerals and gypsum or anhydrite.&nbsp;
Cementation along with compaction are together&nbsp;&nbsp;
known as lithification, which is the process&nbsp;
of converting sediments into sedimentary rock.&nbsp;
We now have sufficient knowledge to start looking&nbsp;
at siliciclastic rocks in more detail. Let’s start&nbsp;&nbsp;
with sandstones. We already know that sandstone&nbsp;
is a rock composed mainly of sand-sized grains,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which make up the rock’s framework. The most&nbsp;
common framework minerals are quartz, followed&nbsp;&nbsp;
by feldspar. Another common framework constituent&nbsp;
are pieces of weathered and eroded rock, called&nbsp;&nbsp;
lithic fragments. For example, the sedimentary&nbsp;
rocks of the Appalachian Basin contain abundant&nbsp;&nbsp;
fragments of metamorphic rock derived from the&nbsp;
erosion of uplifted metamorphic terrane during the&nbsp;&nbsp;
Acadian Orogeny. Geologists classify sandstones&nbsp;
by combining two properties: framework mineralogy,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and the proportion of framework to matrix. A&nbsp;
quaternary diagram can be used to illustrate&nbsp;&nbsp;
the different types of sandstone. Let’s start&nbsp;
by assuming the amount of matrix is zero; these&nbsp;&nbsp;
are known as “arenites”. Sandstones that are very&nbsp;
rich in quartz are called quartz arenites. If the&nbsp;&nbsp;
framework is composed mainly of quartz and lithic&nbsp;
fragments, it is called a lithic arenite, if it&nbsp;&nbsp;
is instead quartz and feldspar, it would be an&nbsp;
arkosic, or feldspathic, arenite. Sandstones with&nbsp;&nbsp;
more than 15% matrix are called wackes (wack-ees),&nbsp;
so if the framework was dominated by quartz and&nbsp;&nbsp;
lithic fragments, it would be classified as a&nbsp;
lithic wacke, and so forth. Sedimentary rocks with&nbsp;&nbsp;
more than 50% matrix are considered mudstones.
Recall from the previous tutorial that feldspar&nbsp;&nbsp;
is unstable in weathering environments and alters&nbsp;
to clay over time. The relative concentration&nbsp;&nbsp;
of stable materials such as quartz, to unstable&nbsp;
materials such as feldspar, is used in conjunction&nbsp;&nbsp;
with the degree of rounding, sorting, and amount&nbsp;
of matrix to determine a sandstone’s “maturity”.&nbsp;&nbsp;
More specifically, maturity is broken down into&nbsp;
two categories, textural and compositional.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Compositionally mature sandstones are depleted in&nbsp;
materials that are thermodynamically unstable in&nbsp;&nbsp;
weathering environments, including most igneous&nbsp;
and metamorphic minerals except for quartz.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Consequently, they tend to be quartz arenites and&nbsp;
quartz wackes, though they can occasionally be&nbsp;&nbsp;
lithic arenites or lithic wackes if the lithic&nbsp;
fragments are quartz-rich, such as chert or&nbsp;&nbsp;
quartzite. Compositional maturity indicates that a&nbsp;
rock’s sediment has spent a great deal of time in&nbsp;&nbsp;
a weathering environment prior to deposition and&nbsp;
burial, sufficient to chemically alter unstable&nbsp;&nbsp;
sediments. We will talk more about chemical&nbsp;
weathering in a later tutorial. Texturally mature&nbsp;&nbsp;
sandstones have well-rounded and well-sorted&nbsp;
grains with little to no matrix mud, indicating&nbsp;&nbsp;
great transport distance from source to burial.
Let’s move on to the coarsest-grained sedimentary&nbsp;&nbsp;
rocks, conglomerates. Conglomerates are&nbsp;
composed of at least 30% gravel-sized&nbsp;&nbsp;
particles. True conglomerates have&nbsp;
well to sub-rounded clasts, whereas the&nbsp;&nbsp;
term “breccia” describes a conglomeritic&nbsp;
rock that is composed of angular clasts.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Since it is relatively uncommon for rocks to&nbsp;
contain mineral grains larger than 2 millimeters,&nbsp;&nbsp;
most conglomerate gravels are composed of lithic&nbsp;
fragments, rather than monocrystalline grains,&nbsp;&nbsp;
as is typically the case for quartz sandstones.&nbsp;
Conglomerates are classified based on the&nbsp;&nbsp;
proportion of framework to matrix, similar to&nbsp;
sandstones. True conglomerates contain very&nbsp;&nbsp;
little matrix and are framework-supported, meaning&nbsp;
that gravel-sized clasts are touching. When a&nbsp;&nbsp;
conglomerate is matrix-supported, it is called&nbsp;
a diamictite, with diamictite meaning “mixed&nbsp;&nbsp;
rock”. Diamictite is a general term for extremely&nbsp;
poorly sorted geologic material in a muddy matrix&nbsp;&nbsp;
and is sometimes used to describe glacial till.&nbsp;
To summarize, conglomerate typically refers to a&nbsp;&nbsp;
clast-supported rock composed of rounded gravel.&nbsp;
Breccia also refers to a clast-supported rock,&nbsp;&nbsp;
but composed of angular gravel. And diamictite&nbsp;
refers to any matrix-supported rock that&nbsp;&nbsp;
contains gravel-sized clasts. The compositional&nbsp;
classification of conglomerates can get a bit&nbsp;&nbsp;
complicated but is generally based on the type&nbsp;
of lithic fragments that comprise the framework&nbsp;&nbsp;
fraction, mainly distinguishing sedimentary,&nbsp;
metamorphic, and igneous clasts. Additionally,&nbsp;&nbsp;
they can be classified based on clast stability,&nbsp;
similar to sandstone maturity. Conglomerates with&nbsp;&nbsp;
greater than 90% stable clasts are said to&nbsp;
be “quartzose”, while those with less than&nbsp;&nbsp;
90% stable clasts are said to be “petromict”.
Last but not least are the mudstones and shales,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which are the finest-grained variety of&nbsp;
siliciclastic rocks. More than half of&nbsp;&nbsp;
all sedimentary rocks are mudstones, compared&nbsp;
to about 20% for sandstones and only 1% for&nbsp;&nbsp;
conglomerates. Recall that mud is a general&nbsp;
term for silt and clay-sized grains. Hence,&nbsp;&nbsp;
mudstones are composed of more than 50%&nbsp;
clay-sized grains, with silt-sized grains&nbsp;&nbsp;
making up most of the rest. Conglomerates, with&nbsp;
their very coarse grains, are deposited in the&nbsp;&nbsp;
highest energy depositional environments, like&nbsp;
rocky beaches and braided streams. At the other&nbsp;&nbsp;
end of the spectrum are mudstones, which can&nbsp;
only be deposited in low energy environments,&nbsp;&nbsp;
where fine-grained sediments are able to settle&nbsp;
out of suspension. The largest mudstone deposits&nbsp;&nbsp;
form in the deep, calm waters of lakes and oceans.
It is not uncommon for clay, silt, and fine sand&nbsp;&nbsp;
to be simultaneously present in a mudstone,&nbsp;
elevating the importance of proper grain size&nbsp;&nbsp;
classification. This can be done accurately with&nbsp;
a petrographic microscope, though the clay-sized&nbsp;&nbsp;
grains will be too small to resolve. Field&nbsp;
geologists have a few tricks for determining&nbsp;&nbsp;
grain size. Mud can be easily distinguished from&nbsp;
sand based on whether grains are visible to the&nbsp;&nbsp;
naked eye, since anything smaller than fine&nbsp;
sand is too small to be seen. So, if we have&nbsp;&nbsp;
a sedimentary rock without visible grains, it&nbsp;
would be classified as a mudstone. Furthermore,&nbsp;&nbsp;
the relative proportions of clay to silt can be&nbsp;
determined by rubbing a small sample over your&nbsp;&nbsp;
teeth to determine the amount of grit present.&nbsp;
If little to no grit is detected, it is probably&nbsp;&nbsp;
nearly pure clay and would be classified in the&nbsp;
field as a claystone. If moderate grit is present,&nbsp;&nbsp;
that will suffice to classify it as a general&nbsp;
mudstone. If a mudstone contains a fraction&nbsp;&nbsp;
of visible fine-grained material, it&nbsp;
would be classified as sandy mudstone.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Siltstone is a more specific type of mudstone,&nbsp;
being mainly composed of silt-sized particles.&nbsp;
Compositionally, mudstones are primarily composed&nbsp;
of clay minerals, with moderate amounts of&nbsp;&nbsp;
silt-sized quartz and feldspar. There are several&nbsp;
types of clay minerals including illite, chlorite,&nbsp;&nbsp;
and kaolinite. They are a product of the chemical&nbsp;
weathering of primary silicate minerals, such as&nbsp;&nbsp;
feldspar, amphibole, and pyroxene. Mudstones can&nbsp;
be quite complex mineralogically, containing minor&nbsp;&nbsp;
amounts of several other minerals, especially&nbsp;
the carbonate, sulfide, and oxide/hydroxide&nbsp;&nbsp;
groups. They can also be quite rich in organic&nbsp;
carbon, making up an enormous fraction of global&nbsp;&nbsp;
source rocks for oil and gas extraction.
The classification of mudstones isn’t as&nbsp;&nbsp;
straightforward as that of sandstones, which can&nbsp;
be easily classified by plotting compositional&nbsp;&nbsp;
and textural properties on ternary diagrams.&nbsp;
Classifying mudstones involves stringing together&nbsp;&nbsp;
descriptive terms. For example, a mudstone that is&nbsp;
rich in organic carbon with abundant fossils would&nbsp;&nbsp;
be classified as a fossiliferous carbonaceous&nbsp;
mudstone. Mudstones rich in organic carbon&nbsp;&nbsp;
tend to be black in color and often contain&nbsp;
pyrite, or FeS2. If a mudstone is fissile,&nbsp;&nbsp;
meaning it easily breaks into planar sheets, it&nbsp;
is called a shale, the term most nongeologists&nbsp;&nbsp;
probably know rather than mudstone. If&nbsp;
a mudstone contains very thin layers,&nbsp;&nbsp;
called laminae, it is said to be laminated.
Another interesting property of mudstone is&nbsp;&nbsp;
that it’s the most radioactive of the sedimentary&nbsp;
rocks. There are a few reasons for this. First,&nbsp;&nbsp;
some clay minerals are rich in potassium,&nbsp;
and 0.01% of potassium is the radioactive&nbsp;&nbsp;
isotope potassium-40. Second, several clay&nbsp;
minerals contain trace amounts of thorium,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which is also radioactive. And three,&nbsp;
organic materials help concentrate uranium.&nbsp;&nbsp;
The association between organic-rich mudstones&nbsp;
and radioactivity is exploited by oil and gas&nbsp;&nbsp;
prospectors, who measure the gamma ray intensity&nbsp;
throughout boreholes to identify likely targets.&nbsp;&nbsp;
Homes built in areas with mudstone bedrock are&nbsp;
often afflicted with high levels of radon gas,&nbsp;&nbsp;
which is part of the decay chain of uranium&nbsp;
and thorium. That covers the classification&nbsp;&nbsp;
of siliciclastic rocks. Let’s move forward and&nbsp;
examine another type of sedimentary rock next.
